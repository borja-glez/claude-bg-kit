# Closing a review round

Read on the Medium path (its step 6) and from `references/task-steps.md` (step 5). `FORGE` means what the skill that sent you here says; `<id>-<n>` is the brief's id (`T-<n>` in a task of an execution), `<WORK>` the task's work folder as the file that sent you here names it, `<path>` is `medium` on the Medium path and `large` in a task of an execution, and `--cwd <worktree>` goes only with a task in a worktree. "Step 3" and "step 7" below are Medium's; in an execution, `references/task-steps.md` step 5 says what each `next` becomes there.

`FORGE review-close --task <id>-<n> --dir <WORK> --path <path> [--cwd <worktree>] --locale <es|en>`: it runs the verdict with the flags review-prep wrote in `review-plan.json` (`--sensitive` goes with security: the diff's zones or `--sensitive`, kept in later rounds; `--perf`), decides the round, logs the minor and `deferred` findings as a ruling and prints `{action, score, next, last, implementer, findings, deferred, invalid, incomplete}`. Exit 3 with `missing` (files of spawned reviewers not there): respawn each with its `spawn` entry and close again when they return. If `invalid` (`invalid: true`) or `incomplete` (a draft, `complete: false`) names a reviewer, respawn it once with the same entry and dir (it picks up its draft) and close again before trusting it (closing a round again counts once). Unusable JSON abstained; when only some findings were dropped, the review keeps its verdict and still scores. Still invalid or incomplete after the respawn: keep the result; when findings were dropped, record them from its raw JSON as a ruling (`FORGE log --kind ruling --task <id>-<n> --data-file <file>`, file: `{"dropped":[...]}`). Then follow `next`; a fix round's implementer is always review-close's `implementer.agent`, never one worked out from the ladder. **Findings to send** is the file in `findings` (`findings-<n>-<round>.json`): the critical and important findings except `deferred` ones, plus the `failures` of every validator with `ok: false` and no `preexisting` in `validators-<n>.json`. `deferred` lists what does not block, with its `residual`; step 7 reports it. For `ask`, `handover` and a NEEDS_CONTEXT fix round, read `references/convergence.md` now.
- `done`: next task.
- `resume`: send the findings to the same implementer (resume it), then repeat from step 3.
- `fresh-stronger`: a fresh implementer, the one printed, with the brief, the `handover`, the report and the findings to send; repeat from step 3.
- `redo`: undo the attempt with `revert` as `references/convergence.md` says (`null`: nothing to undo), then a fresh implementer, the one printed, starts again from the brief another way with the `handover`; repeat from step 3.
- `breaker` or `stalled`: stop the loop. If what is open blocks the work, tell the user and ask; otherwise record the open findings as a ruling (`FORGE log --kind ruling --data-file <file>`) and continue, telling the user at the end.
- `tierUp: true` (from round 3) and `fresh-stronger` are one single step up, not two, and `last: true` sends the attempt to `impl-heavy`: the printed `implementer` already applies both.

After the verdict, when `verify.json` exists, read `references/verify.md` now and follow `## After the verdict`.
A `verify` fail is a finding like any other: its failed checks are in the verdict's critical/important lists and go in **Findings to send**.
